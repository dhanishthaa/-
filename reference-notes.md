# Visual reference findings

The first two supplied references establish an organic visual language rather than literal decorative flowers. The flower image is a close-up pale pink lily with translucent petals, dew droplets, and a soft green field; its emotional value comes from moisture, intimacy, and low-contrast detail. The second reference is deliberately abstract and almost out of focus: a warm blush-and-sage haze with no sharp subject, useful as a full-bleed transition or video-like atmospheric layer.

The correct implementation direction is therefore **realistic but defocused floral atmosphere**, kept at low opacity behind or beside content so it reads like scent in air. It should not become a sticker, border, repeated tile, or high-saturation bloom. Blush, sage, warm ivory, and muted cherry can sit beside the existing ink/cherry palette while the text remains dark on pale haze or ivory/light on ink.

The supplied logo issue is separate from the flower treatment: the current visible mark is too small because the PDF/SVG page ratio makes the wordmark feel reduced inside its plate. The next revision will use a full PNG derived from the supplied logo, without arbitrary cropping, and will scale it as a visible brand plate in the light header, hero, footer, favicon, and admin surfaces.

The third reference confirms that the effect can go darker and greener when used as an atmosphere: a soft pale flower glows against a grainy forest-green field, but the flower itself stays blurred enough to feel photographic rather than illustrative. The fourth shows the strongest fit for the isth canvas: pale ivory ground, blush flowers, muted olive stems, and a soft focus that allows type or a bottle to sit above it. These references support using one pale floral layer in the lifestyle story and one darker green floral layer in a product transition, with no gold star or orbital ornaments.

The fifth and sixth references move toward scattered out-of-focus blossoms and a full-frame peach/ivory haze. They are useful as transition scenes between product groups: the user should feel a lifestyle moment and a sense of air, not read a literal flower catalog. The safest treatment is one or two generated floral plates with a darkened text-safe gradient, very slow parallax, and no repeated patterning.

The seventh reference introduces the strongest natural-lifestyle direction: soft pink and cream flowers against deep living green. It can anchor a single dark editorial passage, but it should be desaturated and used selectively so the existing cherry/ink system remains in control. The supplied PNG preview shows the logo as a small full wordmark on an ivory plate; the revision should preserve the entire wordmark and increase the plate and displayed logo size rather than cropping the source.

The second supplied PNG is a tiny gold four-point star on a deep cherry field. This confirms the gold star is a separate motif, not part of the isth wordmark; it will be removed from all UI and replaced only with whitespace, floral atmosphere, or typographic rhythm.

The third supplied PNG confirms the footer should be quiet and black, with a full light logo plate and the line “Fine fragrance, composed quietly.” The fourth supplied PNG is the strongest homepage target: two women occupy the right two-thirds, the left side is dark and text-safe, the hero title is large white serif with an italic second line, and the top-left logo is a small but clearly visible pale plate. The revised public header will keep this exact contrast logic while using the full supplied PNG wordmark rather than the current cropped treatment.
